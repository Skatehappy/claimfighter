Florida Dispute Guides

🏠 Denied Homeowners Insurance Claim in Florida

Claim-handling deadlines: In Florida, your insurer must acknowledge your claim within 7 calendar days of receiving each claim communication (Fla. Stat. § 627.70131(1)(a)), accept or deny it within 60 days of notice of the claim (§ 627.70131(7)(a)), and pay an accepted claim within 20 days after a settlement agreement is reached (§ 627.4265). The insurer must begin its investigation within 7 days and, for residential property, conduct any physical inspection within 30 days of the proof of loss (§ 627.70131(3)). Overdue payments accrue interest from the date of notice at the statutory rate set by Fla. Stat. § 55.03 (§ 627.70131(7)(a)).

Bad-faith remedies: Florida recognizes statutory first-party bad faith under the civil-remedy statute. Fla. Stat. § 624.155 creates a first-party civil remedy for an insurer's failure to settle in good faith, but you must first file a Civil Remedy Notice (CRN) and give the insurer a 60-day cure period (§ 624.155(3)). If the insurer does not cure within 60 days, you may recover extra-contractual damages caused by the bad faith. Note that SB 76 (2021) and SB 2-A (2022) eliminated one-way attorney's fees in residential property suits and largely banned assignment of benefits.

🔥 Fire Damage Claim Underpayment in Florida

Appraisal rights: Appraisal is contractual rather than statutory in Florida; most property policies contain an appraisal provision that either party may invoke to resolve an amount-of-loss dispute.

Deadline to sue: You generally have 5 years to sue on the insurance contract (Fla. Stat. § 95.11(2)(b)), but you must give notice of a new or reopened property claim within 1 year of the date of loss and any supplemental claim within 18 months (§ 627.70132).
