Illinois Dispute Guides

🏠 Denied Homeowners Insurance Claim in Illinois

Claim-handling deadlines: In Illinois, your insurer must acknowledge your claim within 15 working days of receiving a communication from you (50 Ill. Adm. Code 919.40), accept or deny it within a reasonable time after completing its investigation (50 Ill. Adm. Code 919.50), and pay an accepted claim within 30 days after affirming liability, if the amount is determined and not in dispute (50 Ill. Adm. Code 919.50). If a fire and extended coverage claim remains unresolved more than 75 days after it is reported, or 25 days after proof of loss (whichever is less), the insurer must send you a written explanation of the delay (50 Ill. Adm. Code 919.80). The 215 ILCS 5/155 penalty is the primary statutory consequence of vexatious and unreasonable delay: attorney fees and costs plus an amount up to the greatest of 60% of the amount owed, $60,000, or the excess over the insurer's settlement offer.

Bad-faith remedies: Illinois recognizes statutory remedy only — Illinois does not recognize a separate common-law bad-faith tort. 215 ILCS 5/155 is the exclusive first-party remedy for vexatious and unreasonable claim handling; Illinois courts do not recognize an independent common-law bad-faith tort in first-party cases. A court may award reasonable attorney's fees and costs plus an additional penalty — the greater of 60% of the amount the court finds owed, $60,000, or the amount by which the claim exceeds the insurer's pre-suit offer (215 ILCS 5/155).
