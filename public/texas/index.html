Texas Dispute Guides

🏠 Denied Homeowners Insurance Claim in Texas

Claim-handling deadlines: In Texas, your insurer must acknowledge your claim within 15 days of receiving notice of the claim (Tex. Ins. Code § 542.055), accept or deny it within 15 business days after receiving all items it reasonably requested (§ 542.056), and pay an accepted claim within 5 business days after notifying you the claim is accepted (§ 542.057). Weather-catastrophe deadlines are extended 15 days when the commissioner declares a catastrophe (§ 542.059). A late-paid claim accrues statutory interest of 18% per year, or, in an action to which Chapter 542A applies (forces-of-nature claims), simple interest at the judgment-date rate under Finance Code § 304.003 plus 5% — plus reasonable and necessary attorney's fees (§ 542.060).

Bad-faith remedies: Texas recognizes both statutory and common-law first-party bad faith. Chapter 541 creates a private action for unfair settlement practices, Chapter 542/542A imposes prompt-payment penalties without proof of bad faith, and Texas recognizes a common-law duty of good faith and fair dealing (Arnold v. National County Mutual Fire Ins. Co.). Under Chapter 541 you can recover actual damages, mental-anguish and consequential damages, and treble (3×) damages for a knowing violation, plus attorney's fees. Under Chapter 542 you recover the full delayed amount, statutory interest, and mandatory attorney's fees.

Texas Storm, Hurricane and Roof Claims

Chapter 542A governs first-party claims for damage to real property caused wholly or partly by forces of nature, including hurricane, hail, wind, tornado, flood, wildfire, lightning and rainstorm (§ 542A.001). It applies to a lawsuit on such a claim against the insurer or its adjuster, whether the suit alleges breach of contract, a Chapter 541 unfair-settlement violation or a Chapter 542 prompt-payment violation (§ 542A.002).

Before you sue, you must give written notice no later than the 61st day before filing suit (§ 542A.003(a)). The notice must state the acts or omissions behind the claim, the specific amount you say the insurer owes for the property damage, and the reasonable attorney's fees incurred to date (§ 542A.003(b)). Notice is not required if there is too little time left before limitations runs, or if the action is a counterclaim (§ 542A.003(d)).

After receiving your notice, the insurer has 30 days to send a written request to inspect, photograph or evaluate the property, and the inspection must be completed within 60 days of the notice if reasonably possible (§ 542A.004). An insurer that did not get a compliant notice, or was not given a reasonable chance to inspect, can ask the court to abate the suit (§ 542A.005).

Attorney's fees in a Chapter 542A action are tied to how closely the judgment tracks the amount demanded in your notice: no fees are awarded if the judgment is less than 20% of the amount alleged in the notice, and full reasonable fees are available if it is 80% or more (§ 542A.007). If the notice never stated the specific amount owed, fees incurred after the insurer pleads that defect can be cut off (§ 542A.007(d)). This is why the amount in your demand letter matters.

Late-payment interest in a Chapter 542A action is simple interest at the judgment-date rate under Finance Code § 304.003 plus 5%, rather than the 18% rate that applies otherwise (§ 542.060(c)). Disputes over the amount of a storm loss are often decided through the policy's appraisal clause; the Texas Supreme Court held in Barbara Technologies Corp. v. State Farm Lloyds that paying an appraisal award does not, by itself, shield an insurer from prompt-payment damages.
